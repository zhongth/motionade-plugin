# Remake a reference

Use when the person wants their own film in the structure of a reference video: its cuts, beats, rhythm and soundtrack, told with their brand and their product. It is an adaptation. The reference fixes the skeleton, the person's brand and product supply everything on screen, and you design the mapping between them. A reskin that swaps words and colours on the reference's layouts misses the goal, and so does an original film that keeps only the music.

## Read the reference as a skeleton

Fix the clocks and the cut map, and write each sequence's event model, as [Reference reconstruction](reconstruction.md#fix-the-clocks-and-the-cut-map-first) says. Do not rebuild the reference itself unless the person asks. Then add what a remake keeps:

- **What each Scene does for the viewer:** hook, claim, proof, brand, join or close; the product moment it shows; and its kind of shot, such as a kinetic sentence, a product UI with a cursor, a dot that becomes a mark, a counter, an end card.
- **What runs through the film:** a persistent anchor (a sidebar, a window, a strip), a motif that carries the argument, the colour that marks emphasis.
- **The beats the music marks:** cuts, arrivals, clicks, landings. Keep the soundtrack on the native clock unless the person gives you another.
- **The pictures it shows:** photographs, footage and illustrations, with their frames.

## Learn the brand and the product

Read what the person named with the command, or the folder you work in:

1. **A repository** is the richest source. Read the product's real features, screens, UI components, strings, design tokens, fonts and brand assets. Where you can run the product, capture its real screens at the film's size.
2. **A site.** Read it, then search beyond it for the brand's own material: logo, colours, type, product screens, what it claims. Keep what a source confirms, and mark what you inferred.
3. **A film that is not about a product,** such as lyrics, b-roll or a story: work out its subject from what the person gave.

When the subject, the brand or what the product does is unclear, or there is too little to tell a story with, ask the person before you design. Write down what the product really does and what the film must not claim.

When the reference shows photographs, footage or illustrations, tell the person which ones and ask where theirs should come from: their site, social accounts, videos or repository, files they send, generated images, or native shapes instead. Ask even when they did not mention pictures; many people only notice the question here. Upload what they choose as [Native authoring](authoring.md#media-from-your-own-files) says.

A Text takes only a family the server lists in `fonts`. When the brand's typeface is not listed, choose the nearest listed family and say which one stands in.

## Map roles, not pixels

Write one table the whole film is built from: for each Scene, what the reference does and what ours does.

- **Keep the skeleton.** Keep the events, their frames and the joins. When our content needs a different amount of time, such as a longer typed line, re-pace inside the same window rather than moving the landing.
- **Give each element the role its counterpart has:** the same size, position, attention and direction of travel. The reference's persistent sidebar can become the product's own persistent element; its demo website, a sample made in the product; its status word, the product's own.
- **Design, don't transcribe.** Where our content differs, make the moment as good as the reference is at that moment: hierarchy, spacing, grid, colour discipline. Composition and motion are decided together ([Motion composition](motion-composition.md)).
- **Map colours by role and lightness, not hue.** The reference's accent becomes the brand's accent; its paper and ink become the brand's.
- **Show the product at its best.** Put its real UI, words and behaviour in the moments that prove what it does.
- **Leave nothing of the reference behind:** none of its brand, copy, logos, product or pictures on screen, and none of its words in object or Action names.
- **Record every deliberate departure** from the reference, with its reason.

## Show the brand kit and the storyboard

Before the full build, show the person two things, and wait for their word:

1. **Brand kit:** the mark and wordmark, the palette with the reference colour each one replaces, the typefaces and any stand-in, the product screens and UI pieces the film shows, and the source of each picture.
2. **Storyboard with its story:** the story in a few sentences, then one row per Scene or beat: the reference frame, our composition at that moment, its copy, and what the viewer understands there.

Make the storyboard something they can look at: our compositions as frames rendered from a rough native project, beside the reference's frames, in an image or page in the working folder. Fold their changes into the table before anyone builds.

## Build and review

Build as a reconstruction does: shared components first, then each Scene through its comparison loop, then assembly and joins ([Reference reconstruction](reconstruction.md#fit-recurring-components-before-shots)). Assemble a rough of the whole film and watch it as a contact sheet before detailed work on any Scene. Compare each candidate with the reference frame at the same moment for structure: the same beat, energy and framing role, with our content. Do not chase its pixels; a similarity score means nothing here.

A reviewer who did not build the film judges it against the storyboard and the reference:

- **Blocker:** a principal event missing or off its beat, wrong order, wrong or unreadable copy, a pop or jump at a join, anything of the reference's brand on screen, a claim the product cannot back.
- **Major:** a composition weaker than the reference's at the same moment, UI that does not look like the real product, off-brand colour or type, a dead stretch where the reference moves, motion that fights the reference's character.
- **Minor:** spacing, small timing offsets, simplified detail.

## Report

1. **Structure:** per Scene, whether it keeps the reference's beat and role, and each deliberate departure.
2. **Brand and product:** where each element came from, each stand-in (a typeface, a picture), and what you inferred rather than confirmed. Say whether the soundtrack is the reference's.
3. **Editability and export,** as [Reference reconstruction](reconstruction.md#report) reports them.
